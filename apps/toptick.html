02 / Making one

Three ways to build a list. Most apps give you one.

Typing forty items with two thumbs is why shopping lists end up on the back of an envelope. So TopTick gives you three routes in, and you will use all of them.

Route one

Tap what you need

Open an activity and the items are already there in labelled groups — produce, dairy, frozen, household. Tap the ones you want. A full grocery run takes about forty seconds and no typing at all.

Route two · the one nobody else has

Paste a sentence, get a checklist

Someone texts you “bread, eggs, coffee, dish soap, bananas”. Copy the message, open TopTick, tap once. Five separate, tickable items — not a wall of text you have to read in the aisle. Commas, bullets, numbered lines and line breaks are all understood.

Route three

Start blank, or import someone else’s

Type your own from nothing when the job is unusual. Or take a list somebody shared with you, import it, and it becomes a real checklist on your phone — quantities, units, icon and all.

04 / Sharing

Send the list. They tick. You see it.

Sharing is a plain WhatsApp message — readable by anyone, even without the app. But if they do have TopTick, something better happens.

You share it

Tap Share and pick WhatsApp, email, anything. It goes out as ordinary text carrying the activity’s own icon — an anchor for a ship joining kit, a trolley for the shopping.

They tick it on their phone

They copy the message, open TopTick, tap Import. Now it is a real checklist in their app — quantities, units and all — and they tick items off in their own aisle.

They send it back, and your list updates

TopTick recognises it as the same list and updates yours instead of making a duplicate. Their ticks appear on your copy, with the real time each was done and who did it. Nothing you already ticked is ever undone.

06 / Questions

Before you install.

If it has no internet, how does sharing work?
Your phone’s share sheet does it. TopTick hands the text to WhatsApp, Gmail or whatever you pick, and that app sends it — the same as sharing a photo. TopTick itself never opens a connection, which is why it needs no permission.
What happens to my lists if I change phones?
Settings → Backup to file writes everything to one small file you keep wherever you like. On the new phone, Restore from file brings back every list, tick, quantity and reminder.
Can two of us edit the same list at once?
Not live — that would need a server. Instead: share the list, they tick it in their own copy, send it back, and your list updates rather than duplicating. For a shopping trip it works well; for second-by-second collaboration it does not.
Does it work offline, on a ship, in a basement?
It only works offline. There is no online mode to fall back from. Creating, ticking, reminders and backups all happen on the device with no signal at all.
Which Android versions does it run on?
Android 7.0 (2016) and newer, which covers effectively every phone still in use. The app is about 4 MB.
Is any of this going to start costing money?
Every feature described on this page is free, with no account and no time limit, and stays that way. If advanced features are added later they will be a one-time purchase — never a subscription — you will be able to try them before paying, and a full refund is available for 30 days.
